Option 3 — Just open it

The first-visit page is one big target: open a pattern or a picture. stitchx already detects file types, so the "Convert or import?" choice goes away. A question only comes up when it's needed: after someone picks a photo.

Why this direction

  • The app can already tell files apart. ImportEngine.classifyFileForCreate() sorts a file into pattern, image or unsupported. Today Home still makes people choose a tile first, then warns them when they pick the wrong one. One "Choose a file" button removes that choice and the warnings.
  • Ask at the moment it matters. The "photo of a printed chart" problem only affects people who pick a photo. They get one plain question ("What's in this picture?") and, only if they say "A printed chart", the explanation and a way out. Everyone else never sees the warning.
  • Phone first. Returning users mostly carry on stitching from a phone. A bottom tab bar (Home, Projects, Stash, Stats) puts navigation under the thumb, and the "Pick up where you left off" shelf makes Continue a single tap on a big picture of the work.
  • Actions in a sheet, not a row. Each project card has one tap target (Continue). Everything else (Edit, Export, Update chart, Details) is in a sheet that slides up, with a line of explanation for the rare ones. That's how phone apps handle secondary actions, and it can't overflow.
  • Sentences instead of dashboards. "At a glance" gives three plain sentences that each link somewhere. That's enough to bring people back to the Stash and Stats pages without building a second dashboard on Home.
  • Projects get their own page. Home stops trying to be the library. The shelf shows unfinished work and the Projects tab holds everything, so nothing gets silently cut off.

Walkthrough findings it fixes

  • F1One-line pitch and a privacy line under the drop zone.
  • F3 F4 F5One "Choose a file" button instead of tiles plus "+ New project"; no tab/content mismatch.
  • F6 F7The paper-chart warning appears only after someone picks a photo and says it's a printed chart.
  • F8"Try the demo pattern" is a first-class choice that opens the real Tracker.
  • F9 F10Labelled sidebar (desktop) or tab bar (phone); backup and preferences live under Settings.
  • F11Stash and Stats are real destinations, with one-line hooks on Home.
  • F12One tap target per card; everything else in an action sheet.
  • F13 F14Big previews with progress; the full list moves to Projects, with a count in the nav.
  • F15 F16Designed at 390 px first. The active project leads the shelf instead of appearing in three places.

Trade-offs

  • The biggest change to the app's structure: a new nav model (sidebar or tab bar) and a Projects page. That's more work than Options 1 and 2, and it touches the shared header on every page.
  • A single "Choose a file" button hides what stitchx can do. Someone who has no file yet sees only "blank canvas" and "demo" as alternatives. The pitch line has to do the explaining.
  • The photo question adds one step to the Convert path for the most common new-user goal. It's one tap, and it replaces three warnings.

Build notes

  • Routing already exists: CreatePanel.startFile() sends charts to ImportEngine.importAndReview and pictures to the Convert handoff. The photo question goes between classify() and handOffImage().
  • The page-wide drop target is the existing onDrop handler moved from the Create tab to the whole page.
  • The Projects page can start as manager.html's Pattern library, already reachable today.
