Option 1 — Ask what they have

The page asks one question in the user's own words, "What are you starting with?", and gives one path per answer. Returning users get a single obvious "Continue stitching" and a picture of every project.

Why this direction

  • New users know what they have, not which tool they need. Maya has "a PDF from Etsy" and Tom has "a photo of my dog". Naming the cards after what's in their hands removes the "Convert vs Import" puzzle that the current page fixes with three warnings.
  • A one-sentence pitch and a privacy line come first. The current page never says what stitchx is. "Free, no account, never uploaded" is stitchx's biggest difference from other pattern sites, and it sat inside a collapsed panel.
  • Warn once, where it applies. The note about photos of printed charts appears in "Which files work?", next to the file types it explains, instead of three times on the page.
  • A real demo, not a toy. The demo opens the actual Stitch Tracker on a tiny pattern, so what people practise on is what they'll use.
  • Introduce the vocabulary. "How stitchx fits together" names the Pattern Creator, Stitch Tracker and Stash Manager once, so those words make sense when they show up later.
  • Returning users: one primary button. "Continue stitching" is the only filled button on the page. Edit sits beside it. Export, Update chart and Details move into a More menu, because they're rare.
  • The same three cards come back at the bottom for returning users, in compact form, so what people learned on day one still works.

Walkthrough findings it fixes

  • F1Headline, pitch, privacy promise and "How stitchx fits together".
  • F2No "ready to stitch?" on first visit; "Welcome back" only for returning users.
  • F3 F4No tabs and no duplicate "+ New project". The three cards are the only way in.
  • F5 F6 F7Intent-named cards of equal weight; file formats and the paper-chart note live in one "Which files work?" panel.
  • F8 F9Demo opens the real Tracker; advanced-tool text removed from Home.
  • F10 F11Header is Home, Stash, Stats, Help and More, all labelled. Stash and Stats go straight to their full pages instead of thin tabs.
  • F12One primary action per project; the rest behind More.
  • F13 F14Thumbnail, size, progress bar and status on every card; status filters; a clear route to the full library.
  • F15Two-column card grid on phones; no rows of four buttons to overflow.
  • F16Home drops the header project switcher; the Continue card is the one place the active project appears.

Trade-offs

  • A longer first-visit page: four sections instead of one row of tiles. It reads top to bottom, but people who already know what they want have to scroll past the pitch.
  • Taking Stash and Stats out of Home means a click to a separate page to see them. The thin tabs weren't adding anything, but anyone who liked the at-a-glance numbers loses them. Option 2 keeps them.
  • The card grid needs a pattern thumbnail. The project summary rows already have a thumbnail field; it would need filling in on save.

Build notes

  • The cards reuse CreatePanel's two file pickers and the blank-canvas link unchanged.
  • Progress and size come from pct and dimensions on the rows listProjects() already returns.
  • The demo needs a seeded project flagged isDemo so Stats and Sync can ignore it.
